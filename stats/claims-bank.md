# Stats Claims Bank

Every quantitative claim we make in pitches, on the website, or in marketing — with the primary source. If a claim isn't on this page, don't make it.

Source rule: only cite primary sources (the org that actually ran the study). Don't cite a blog post citing a blog post. Where a stat is folklore without a primary source, **do not use it** — flag instead.

---

## Tier 1 — Use freely (primary-sourced, recent)

### Reviews are the #2 ranking factor in Google's local pack
- **Claim:** Reviews account for 20% of Google local pack ranking weight, second only to Google Business Profile signals (32%).
- **Source:** BrightLocal — Google Local Algorithm and Ranking Factors (2026). https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/
- **Use:** Lead stat in pitch. Justifies why reviews specifically matter (vs. social, vs. paid).

### Top-3-ranked local businesses average 47 Google reviews
- **Claim:** Local businesses ranked in Google's top 3 positions average 47 Google reviews vs. ~38 for positions 7-10.
- **Source:** BrightLocal — Google Reviews Study. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/google-reviews-study/
- **Use:** Anchor for the "you need 60+ to compete" pitch math. The 9-review gap between top-3 and bottom-3 is what we're closing.

### Consumer review-trust thresholds
- **Claim 1:** 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.
- **Claim 2:** 31% only use businesses with 4.5+ stars (up from 17% in 2025).
- **Claim 3:** 68% require 4+ stars (up from 55% in 2025).
- **Claim 4:** 74% only care about reviews from the past 3 months.
- **Source:** BrightLocal — Local Consumer Review Survey 2026. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/
- **Use:** "Why your old reviews don't matter as much as you think." Justifies the recency angle in our reporting.

### 5-star vs 3-star CTR in local pack
- **Claim:** In Google Local Pack, 5-star listings earn 69% of clicks vs. 44% for 3-star (~57% relative lift).
- **Source:** BrightLocal — Review Search Click-Through Study. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/review-search-click-through-study/
- **Use:** "Stars determine clicks even at the same ranking position."

### Reply rate matters
- **Claim:** 88% of consumers would use a business that replies to all of its reviews vs. 47% for a business that doesn't respond.
- **Source:** BrightLocal — Local Consumer Review Survey 2024. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2024/
- **Use:** Justifies why the reply-management piece of our service matters (not just acquisition).

### Local Pack click distribution by position
- **Claim (no LSAs in market):** Local Pack #1 = 17.6% CTR, #2 = 15.4%, #3 = 15.1%.
- **Claim (with LSAs in market):** Local Pack #1 = 16.1%, #2 = 10.4%, #3 = 2.3%.
- **Sources:** First Page Sage — Google CTR by Position. https://firstpagesage.com/reports/google-click-through-rates-ctrs-by-ranking-position/ + BrightLocal — Local Services Ads Click Study. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/
- **Use:** When LSAs are present in the spa's metro, the gap from #3 to #1 is ~7×. Confirm whether LSAs run in the prospect's city before pitching this number.

### Conversion lift from star improvement
- **Claim:** Improving from 3.5 → 3.7 stars year-over-year is associated with ~120% conversion growth — the largest jump of any star-rating tier.
- **Source:** BrightLocal — Google Business Profile Conversion Factors. https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-conversion-factors/
- **Use:** Only applies to spas currently below 4.0 stars. Skip when pitching spas already at 4.5+.

### GBP traffic baseline
- **Claim:** Average local business GBP gets 1,260 views/month (943 Search + 317 Maps), with ~5% of views resulting in an action (call, click, directions). ~63 actions/month for the typical business.
- **Source:** BrightLocal — Google My Business Insights Study. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/google-my-business-insights-study/
- **Use:** Baseline for the revenue math. The spa's actual numbers may vary — pull their GBP Insights if possible.

### Med spa cost per acquired patient
- **Claim:** Med spa cost per acquired patient = $285 (average, n=8 practices in dataset).
- **Source:** First Page Sage — Average Patient Acquisition Cost 2026. https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/average-patient-acquisition-cost/
- **Use:** Frames our service ROI vs. paid acquisition. Caveat the small sample on the call.

### Med spa patient lifetime value
- **Claim:** 5-year patient LTV by treatment: Botox average $9,000 (premium $12K); Dermal Fillers average $7,500 (premium $22K); Body Contouring $6K-$10K; Weight Loss $18K (premium $30K).
- **Source:** Dean Garland Insights — Lifetime Value of Med Spa Treatments. https://www.deangarland.com/insights/lifetime-value-of-med-spa-treatments/
- **Use:** The denominator that makes the revenue math obvious. Industry consultancy, not peer-reviewed — flag if pressed.

## Tier 2 — Use with explicit caveats

### 5-9% revenue lift per 1-star rating increase
- **Claim:** Independent businesses see a 5-9% revenue lift per 1-star online rating increase.
- **Source:** Michael Luca, Harvard Business School Working Paper 12-016 (2011, revised 2016). https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/12-016_a7e4a5a2-03f9-490d-b093-8f951238dba2.pdf
- **Caveat to disclose:** Study is on independent restaurants using Yelp, not med spas using Google. Mechanism (online rep → consumer choice) is the same; magnitude is a benchmark not a derived number for aesthetics. Cite as: "the closest peer-reviewed study on the mechanism."
- **Use:** When pressed on causality of the revenue claim. Only peer-reviewed source available.

### Above-average review counts → 82% more revenue
- **Claim:** Small businesses with above-average review counts (>83) see 82% more annual revenue than below-average peers. 200+ reviews → 2× revenue. 9+ reviews in past 90 days → 52% more revenue.
- **Source:** Womply — analysis of 200,000+ US small businesses. https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20190723005207/en/Womply-Study-Suggests-Bad-Google-Reviews-Costlier-to-Small-Businesses-Than-Yelp-or-Facebook + Search Engine Land coverage: https://searchengineland.com/review-counts-matter-more-to-local-business-revenue-than-star-ratings-according-to-study-320271
- **Caveat to disclose:** Womply explicitly states correlation, not causation. Well-managed businesses likely both manage reviews better AND earn more revenue. Don't pitch as causal.
- **Use:** As motivation, never as a prediction of the prospect's outcome.

### Google's largest review penalty is for 1-1.5 star ratings
- **Claim:** 1-1.5 star Google rating → 33% less revenue (vs. -19% for Yelp, -9% for Facebook).
- **Source:** Same Womply study above.
- **Caveat:** Same correlation/causation caveat.
- **Use:** Not relevant for active spa pitches (they're rarely sub-2-star). Useful in long-form content.

### 97% of consumers use reviews to guide purchase decisions
- **Source:** BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026. https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/
- **Use:** Tier 1 stat, listed here only because it's broad-vertical not med-spa-specific.

## Tier 3 — Verifiable case-study claims (cite when relevant, don't claim as our own)

### Sona Dermatology / Birdeye case study
- **Claim:** Sona Dermatology & MedSpa achieved 496% Google review volume growth in 18 months using Birdeye, reached 4.4-star average, and hit #1-2 in relevant local searches.
- **Source:** Birdeye case study. https://birdeye.com/resources/case-studies/sona-med-spa/
- **Important:** No revenue figure published. We can cite the review-growth result as proof the mechanism works; we cannot extrapolate a dollar figure.
- **Use:** "Med spas using review-acquisition services have hit 496% review growth in 18 months — and that was a competitor's tool. We do this for less."

### Solutionreach dermatology case study
- **Claim:** A dermatology clinic earned an extra $250,000 in recall revenue, booked ~800 recall appointments, and gained 150 Google + Facebook reviews using Solutionreach.
- **Source:** Solutionreach case study. https://www.solutionreach.com/case-study/dermatology-clinic-gets-250k-in-recall-revenue-150-online-reviews
- **Caveat:** Does not disclose time period or attribute the $250K specifically to reviews vs. recall.
- **Use:** Adjacent vertical (derm not med spa), use sparingly.

## DO NOT USE — folklore without primary source

These claims appear repeatedly in agency marketing copy but I could not find a primary source. If the founders use them, they will be exposed under sales scrutiny.

- ❌ "The average med spa has 40 Google reviews." — No primary source. Pull the spa's actual local market data instead.
- ❌ "Med spas convert 50-80% of consultations to first procedures." — No primary source found. Industry copy claims this. Don't use.
- ❌ "88% of consumers trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations." — Often misattributed. Use the BrightLocal LCRS 2024 figure (88% would use a business that replies to reviews vs 47% that doesn't) — different stat, real source.
- ❌ "Reviews from 6+ months ago carry zero ranking weight." — Folklore. The real stat is BrightLocal: 74% of consumers only care about reviews from the past 3 months (different — that's consumer-trust, not Google's algorithm).
- ❌ "1-star Google review costs you X bookings." — Always made-up specific numbers. Use the Luca 5-9% revenue per star (with caveat) instead.

## Soft research to verify per pitch

These need to be pulled fresh per prospect, never used as global claims:

1. **Top 3 competitor review counts in the prospect's metro** — pull manually from Google Maps before the call. The single most powerful number in the pitch.
2. **Whether LSAs run in the prospect's city** — changes the local pack CTR math from "1.17× lift" to "7× lift." Check by Googling "med spa near me" from a VPN'd location.
3. **The prospect's actual current GBP review count and star rating** — pull before the call. Don't ask them what it is.
4. **The prospect's GBP "Insights" data** — only available to them, but ask: "What does your GBP Insights tab show for monthly views?" If they know, anchor the math on their actual number; if not, use the 1,260 BrightLocal benchmark with the caveat "this is the average — yours may be higher or lower."

## How to use this in a pitch

The pitch is a stack: a single hard fact, followed by the gap, followed by the math.

1. **Hard fact:** "Reviews are the #2 ranking factor for Google's local pack — 20% of the algorithm." (BrightLocal 2026)
2. **Gap:** "Top 3 [city] med spas average 67 Google reviews. You have 22." (Pulled fresh)
3. **Math:** "Top-ranked local businesses average 47 reviews. At your med-spa Botox patient LTV of $9,000 (Dean Garland), even 5 incremental new patients/month is $540K in 5-year booked LTV." (Tier 1 + Tier 1)
4. **Risk reversal:** "$499 setup, $35 per published review, no contract. If we don't close the gap to your top 3 competitors in 90 days, you pay nothing beyond setup."

That's the entire pitch. Four sentences, four sources, one ask.
